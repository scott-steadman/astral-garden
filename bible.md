# Astral Garden Bible

The shared multiverse, the Astral Garden, in which several storylines are told in different formats. This file holds the setting every storyline shares, organized by topic. Each storyline keeps its own format, cast and story notes in its own `storyline.md` file. Writing guidance, the storyline index, open questions and story hooks are in `storylines.md`.

---

## Premise

A D&D 5e multiverse with Iain Banks' Culture flavor.

## The astral sea

**Laws of reality.** Everything and everyone, gods included, is bound by the laws of reality, which encompass the astral sea itself. All planes are created in the astral sea, and within those laws a plane's creator *can* set the plane's own laws (see Gods, Planes' laws).

**The astral sea** contains everything. Scattered through it are planar systems: one or more planes in close proximity, or a single plane sitting alone somewhere in the astral sea.

**Planes.** A plane is a world created in the astral sea and set apart from it.
- **Kinds:** a plane where mortals live is a prime plane. Each god also has a home plane, usually near its creations and often in the same system (see Gods, Home plane).
- **Barrier:** every plane is surrounded by a barrier that protects it from the astral sea. A prime plane's barrier is powered by the life dwelling on it: plants, animals and ensouled beings. A god's home plane's barrier is powered by its god.
- **Dead planes:** a plane whose barrier is gone is exposed to the astral sea and decays slowly, and part of that decay is breaking apart into debris.

**Surviving the astral sea.** Unlike D&D's Astral Plane, beings in the astral sea still need to eat and drink to stay alive. The astral sea is breathable.

**Propulsion.** In the astral sea, most things move by will, as in D&D's Astral Plane.
- **Ensouled ships** move by their own will: the soul is the helm, a big part of why ensouled ships matter.
- **Organic ships** swim the sea naturally, as the creatures they are. They don't move by will, so psychic phenomena don't affect their propulsion, though they may still affect their minds.
- **Manufactured ships** need a helm, a seat that turns a spellcaster's magic into motion (as in Spelljammer). It costs the caster spells and wears them down on long voyages.

**Psychic phenomena.** The astral sea has no everyday weather, but it has rare psychic phenomena, its version of Star Trek's ion storms. Because things move by will, these are the sea's real hazards:
- **Psychic winds**: storms that scramble the will. Ensouled ships lose their way, helm casters are overwhelmed, and *sending* calls break up. Organic ships keep swimming, but their minds may be shaken.
- **Becalming**: dead zones where will moves nothing. Other ships sit stranded until they drift out or are towed, and supplies run down; organic ships can swim out, and often do the towing.
- The River Styx is a psychic wind (see Fiends).
- Others can be invented as stories need them.

**Travel.** To reach a plane you've never visited you must sail the astral or be summoned by someone there. Once there, you can craft a totem from local materials; later, a shift uses the totem to bring the holder and companions back.

## Gods

- **Gardeners.** "God" is short for gardener. A god is an immortal who creates races, for reasons known only to itself.
- **Rare and huge.** Gods are mostly offstage, felt through worshippers, their planes and divine magic. A god appearing in person is a major event.
- **Home plane.** Each god has one home plane. The god's soul powers its barrier (see The astral sea, Planes). How the plane looks, dark or bright, is only the god's taste. A god can plane shift to any plane it's associated with (its worshippers' planes, sandbox planes it helped make).
- **Killable.** Gods are ageless but can be killed. Killed away from home, a god is banished there and reforms (see Souls, Banishment). Killed on its home plane, its soul is destroyed for good and the plane's barrier disappears: the plane decays slowly and breaks apart, and how decayed it is shows how long the god has been dead.
- **Losing a home plane.** Destroying a god's home plane doesn't kill the god, but it leaves the god homeless until it builds a new one:
  - **Stranded souls:** whole souls that choose to languish with that god have no home plane to go to. They drift in the astral sea instead, lost, unprotected and easy prey for demons hunting souls.
  - **No safe return:** a homeless god killed anywhere dies for good.
- **Kinds of prime planes:**
  - **Single-race planes:** one god or pantheon and one people.
  - **Multi-race planes:** made by one creator.
  - **Sandbox planes:** several gods jointly create a plane and play a kind of game, each creating races, with intervention allowed or forbidden by rules they agreed to beforehand (most multi-deity AD&D campaign settings are like this).
- **Ascension.** Mortals can become immortal and attain godhood. Ascension sunders the god's soul. The god trades the soul's undying return for power, so gods can't have children with one another (two sundered souls can't conceive), and a slain god is gone for good. A god can still have a child with a mortal, whose whole soul makes conception possible.
- **Planes' laws.** Within the laws of reality, a god sets the laws of the planes it creates. Some of those laws can only be set at creation:
  - **Barring souls:** a plane's creators can bar sundered souls, whole souls, or both, from entering it.
    - **Only at creation:** the bar is part of the plane's laws, so it can't be added later. Older or careless planes stay open.
    - **Invitation overrides it:** a barred soul can't enter on its own, but someone already on the plane can summon or invite it in. This is why fiends work through mortals' bargains and summonings, and why a fiend-proof plane falls only by betrayal from inside.
  - **Wishes:** whether a wish's reality-altering effects work on a plane is part of that plane's laws, set at creation. A wish's reality-altering effects never work in the astral sea itself, since its laws are the laws of reality, which bind everyone.
  - **Banishment periods:** how long a banished sundered soul is barred from a plane is also set at its creation (see Souls, Banishment).
- **The Deck of Many Things** came out of one god's study of planar creation and of setting a plane's laws. It manipulates the reality of the plane it was made on, so a deck made on one plane won't work on another.

## Souls

- **Sovereignty.** Gods create souls to animate their races, but souls are sovereign: they have free will, exist for their own sake, and are not owned by the gods who made them. The one way around that sovereignty is a soul's true name (see True names).
- **Whole and sundered souls.** A soul is either whole or sundered. A sundered soul is cut off from the part of a soul that makes new life.
  - **Fiends** are beings with sundered souls (see Fiends). Gods are sundered too, by ascension.
  - **Magic:** sundered souls can't worship or use soul magic, only pacts and Arcana (see Magic).
  - **Origin:** the original fiends were made sundered by gods, then discarded or ignored as a failed experiment because they couldn't reproduce. Some newer gods still make fiends while learning how to create souls. Some souls that started whole become sundered through a process not yet specified.
- **Making souls.** Souls are made two ways:
  - **Reproduction:** quick, but a whole soul is needed to reproduce. Two whole souls can create a new soul, and so can a sundered soul paired with a whole one; two sundered souls (fiend and fiend, god and god, god and fiend) cannot. A sundered parent is where cambions and tieflings come from, so fiends value mortals willing to have children with them.
  - **Crafted by gods** ("gardening"): slow.
- **Death of a whole soul.** Whole souls are immortal and don't die with the body. When the body dies, the soul chooses its own fate:
  - **Die permanently.**
  - **Assimilate:** return to its god and merge with it, which effectively destroys the soul as an individual.
  - **Languish:** dwell on its god's home plane (if the god has lost it, the soul drifts in the astral sea instead; see Gods) until the god has another use for it (such as implanting it in a new body during conception; see Soul deals below) or until the soul decides to assimilate.
- **Soul deals with gods.** A god may make a deal with a whole soul to place it in a body for one of the god's games (see Gods, sandbox planes). Two ways in:
  - **Implantation:** the soul enters a new body during conception and is born. Its memories are suppressed while in that body and return when the host dies.
  - **Reincarnation:** the soul is placed in a body with its memories intact.
- **Banishment.** A sundered soul killed away from its home plane is not destroyed. It is banished back to its home plane and reforms there, and it can't return to the plane where it was killed for a period of time set by that plane's laws when the plane was created. Killed on its home plane, a sundered soul dies for good.
- **True names.** Every soul has a true name. Souls do not know their true names inherently; they must discover them. A being who utters a soul's true name removes that soul's free will for a period of time. It is the one way around a soul's sovereignty, so a true name is the most dangerous secret a being can have.
  - **Gods know the true names of the souls they craft.** Nobody automatically knows the true name of a soul born through reproduction.
  - **Sundered and whole names differ.** Sundered souls (fiends and gods) have true names too, and a suffix in the name marks which kind of soul it is. A whole soul's name ends in a closing sound, the seal, the part of the name that marks a soul able to conceive. A sundered name has no seal; it is cut off, ending abruptly. Sundering a soul (by ascension or otherwise) cuts the seal from its name. Anyone versed in true names can hear the difference.
  - **Implanted** souls do not know their true names while in the host (memories suppressed). Reincarnated souls know theirs only if they already did.
  - **Your own name is easier to find, but not easy.** A soul finds its own through long self-knowledge (meditation, a monk's discipline, a paladin's oath). Another soul's name takes hard outside work: research, trickery, or a god who knows it.
  - **Knowing your own name cuts both ways.** A soul that knows its own true name can resist: when someone uses it, the soul can recognize it, fight back, and shorten their hold. But a name held in the mind can be read by a telepath, so knowing it makes you a target (which is why mind-shields matter).

## Magic

Magic has four sources:
- **Divine magic**: comes from the god a soul chooses to worship (clerics). It is produced by worship and prayer and consumed by spells. It only works on "deified" or "accorded" planes unless stored in a holy symbol or magic item.
- **Soul magic**: comes from one's own soul (a paladin's oath, divine soul sorcerers).
- **Pact magic**: comes from a higher being, not necessarily a god, that a warlock makes a pact with.
- **Arcana** (wizard magic): a fundamental force of reality, like gravity and electromagnetism, the same force gods use to create planes and manipulate reality. Works everywhere except anti-magic zones. Can be stored in magic items.

**Sundered souls can't worship, but can make pacts.** Divine and soul magic both need a whole soul, so fiends and gods can use only pact magic and Arcana.
- **No fiend clerics:** fiends turn to pacts instead, which is why warlocks and fiend patrons are everywhere in their world.
- **Gods don't worship gods:** pantheons are alliances held together by pacts, not hierarchies.
- **Sundering costs faith:** a whole soul that becomes sundered loses its divine and soul magic at once.
- **Tieflings and cambions** have whole souls, so they can worship.

## The Astral Federation and Fleet

The Astral Federation is like Starfleet: a young idealistic alliance with ranks and duty where crews make the calls.

- Founded in the Rosste planar system by beings interested in exploration, trade and mutual defense.
- **Calendar.** Decimal: 10 days a week, 10 weeks a month, 10 months a year (1,000 days). Founding of the Federation is day one of year one. Stardates are `year.day`, so 1.693 is day 693 of year 1. Supplemental entries use `.2`.
- **Navigation.** Federation charts use relative coordinates with Rosste at the origin, 0.0.0.
  - **Coordinates:** three numbers, x.y.z, in leagues, a decimal unit to match the calendar. A system might sit at 412.-87.36.
  - **Drift:** planes drift, so each chart entry also gives the plane's drift in leagues per day and the stardate it was measured, for example "Toril 1203.-44.910, drift 2.0.-1, @ 1.693". A navigator works out the current position from these. Sudden changes, such as an abyssal anchor pulling a plane or the Styx shifting course, break the prediction, so old charts can be dangerously wrong.
  - **Headings:** for maneuvering, ships use a bearing and an elevation from their own position ("heading 040 mark 12"). Coordinates find a system; headings steer the ship.
  - **Devils** chart the same way, but with the First Hell at the origin and their own units, so converting between Federation and Hell charts takes work.
  - **Demons** keep no charts.
- **Communication is instantaneous over any distance** via the *sending* spell or upcast variants. Table:

  | Level | Effect | Duration | Participants |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 3rd | *Sending*: one 25-word message and reply | instant | 2 |
  | 4th | Open channel | 1 minute | 4 |
  | 5th | Conference call | 1 hour | 8 |
  | 6th | Conference call | 8 hours | 16 |
  | 7th | Conference call | 1 day | 32 |
  | 8th | Conference call | 10 days (one Federation week) | 64 |
  | 9th | Permanent conference call | permanent | any number attuned to its focus |

  - Participant counts double with each level. A captain's logbook, which copies itself to its twin at HQ, is a permanent 9th-level link built into the ship. Fleet HQ runs permanent channels to each ship; ships can open conference calls among themselves.
- **Ranks** (Starfleet-style, naval): Captain, Commander, Lieutenant Commander, Lieutenant, Ensign; enlisted are Senior Crewmate and Crewmate (the same for every species). Informally, crewmates get affectionate crew-plus-species nicknames, like "skipper" for a captain: a gnome is a "crewgnome", an elf a "crewelf", a dwarf a "crewdwarf". They're used fondly, never on official logs. "Acting" can go in front of any rank when someone fills a role above their own. Department heads are job titles, not ranks: Chief Religionist, Chief Arcanist, Chief Tinkerer, Security Chief (each also holds a rank, e.g. a Chief Arcanist who is a Lieutenant Commander).
- Away teams are designated by the captain (numbers, names, colors, anything), and the scheme tells readers something about that captain. Teams wear earpiece communicators that grant the effect of the *tongues* spell: the wearer understands any spoken language, and anyone who speaks a language understands the wearer. Delegations wear earpieces that provide both *tongues* and mind-shielding; the shield warms under telepathic pressure.
- **Instant travel** comes in three kinds:
  - **Shifting**: instantaneous movement between ships and/or planes (an ensouled ship shifting an away team down to a plane, or a drone shifting itself back aboard, usually via a magic item). Shifting to a plane needs a totem made from its materials (see The astral sea, Travel), so before a ship can shift an away team down, it usually portals a drone in to gather totem components.
  - **Teleporting**: instantaneous movement between two locations on the same plane or ship.
  - **Portaling** (also called a dimension door): opening a passageway, a wormhole, between ships and/or planes that other beings pass through. Upcasting the *dimension door* can make the portal larger (big enough for multiple beings or ships to pass through), longer-lasting, or both. Ensouled ships express themselves through their portals: how a ship's dimension doors appear and what they look like is a personal signature.

### Ships

- **Ships** come in three kinds. Organics: gargantuan astral creatures who volunteered; three signed the founding document. Manufactured: built and crewed vessels. Ensouled: manufactured ships voluntarily animated by a person's soul. Organics and ensouled ships are commissioned officers with full rights.
  - The Federation is young, so there are few organic and ensouled ships so far. Ships vary: some souls lived long lives before joining, others are young.
- Ships train with captain and crew for a month, then go on five-year exploration missions. Organic ships departed first; the first manufactured and ensouled ships launched a few months later.
- **Ensouled ships.** Joining the Fleet is decided by the living being in advance: when it dies, a facilitator captures its soul in a phylactery and carries it to its new vessel (a ship, or another being). From the phylactery, the soul attunes to the ship and controls it through that attunement (see Attunement below). Later in the timeline, the Federation recognizes the risk of phylacteries and learns to reincarnate a soul directly into a biological ship, which keeps its memories with nothing to steal or smash. After that, the living being chooses which it wants, a phylactery or reincarnation, so phylactery ships and reincarnated ones serve side by side.
- **Attunement.** A ship's soul controls everything it uses through attunement: first its own hull, then its drones and avatars.
  - **Cap:** a soul can be attuned to only so many drones and avatars at once, and the number grows with the ship's age and experience.
  - **Time:** attuning takes a while, so a ship can't pick up a strange drone and use it on the spot.
  - **Security:** no one can take over an attuned drone or avatar without first breaking the attunement.
  - **Hazards:** psychic winds and anti-magic zones can disrupt attunement and leave a drone or avatar cut off.
- **Avatars** are a ship's eyes, hands and voice off-ship, like the Culture's ship avatars. They are organic bodies grown without a soul (never conceived), and they don't have to be humanoid. Normally the ship controls them remotely, through attunement. How a ship's avatar looks, moves and talks is another way it expresses its personality (as with its portals).
  - **Moving the phylactery (emergencies):** the phylactery can be detached from the hull and attached to an avatar or a drone (on an avatar, worn as a necklace, for example). The soul goes where the phylactery goes, but it stays attuned to its hull and keeps control of it.
  - **"Ancillary"** is the term for evil ships' version: bodies taken from captured beings and slaved to the ship, as in *Ancillary Justice*.
- **Drones** are tinkered or magical devices that ships use: scouts, probes, tools and couriers, with no soul or personality of their own.

## Fiends

- A fiend is any being with a sundered soul that takes other souls for its own ends. That makes it evil, and sets it apart from gods, who create and nurture souls. By alignment: devils are lawful, demons chaotic, daemons neutral.
- **The Blood War**, as in D&D. The devils are the Klingons and are at war with the demons, who are the Borg.
- **Why fiends need souls.** Fiends cannot make souls on their own, yet devils and demons need them as troops for the Blood War: devils bargain for souls, and demons capture them, indenturing the souls of beings they kill.
- **Demons** (the Abyss): chaotic evil, Borg-like. At war with the devils.
  - **Structure:** the Abyss is a single planar system; each assimilated plane becomes one of its layers.
  - **Assimilation, not contracts:** unlike the Hells, the Abyss absorbs planes of any kind (prime planes, gods' home planes, whole planar systems) as new layers. Their greatest target is the Hells themselves; the Styx carries their hordes there.
  - **Abyssal anchors** are the means by which demons pull a plane through the astral sea to the Abyss for assimilation. Demons don't assimilate planes without barriers, such as a slain god's decaying home plane. Abyssal anchors don't work on the Hells, so the demons can only take them by war.
  - **Harbingers** are how demons find planes. Few demons can sustain themselves in the open astral sea, but harbingers can. Demons scatter them by the thousands, like dandelion seeds. Most never find anything, since the astral sea is mostly empty.
    - **Outside the barrier:** a harbinger can't cross a plane's barrier, so it works from outside, reaching in psychically for a would-be warlock. It deceives rather than bargains, posing as a fey, a celestial or a dead ancestor, or promising power to defeat one's enemies. Later it convinces the warlock to summon the demon who created it.
    - **The summons is the invitation:** the summons carries the demon through the barrier, past any law barring fiends (see Gods, Planes' laws). The demon brings an abyssal anchor and opens a portal for the horde to follow.
    - **Ships have no barrier**, so a harbinger that comes across one can simply attack and try to board it.
  - **The count:** somewhere in the 600s, not yet 666. Demons believe that when the Abyss reaches 666 layers it will become whole, and their sundered souls will be made whole with it. They could then create souls of their own instead of taking them. It is a belief, not a known fact. It may be true, a lie a demon lord tells to keep the hordes moving, or something worse.
- **The River Styx** is a psychic wind in the astral sea that blows along a changing course between the Abyss and the First Hell, reversing direction every 66 days.
  - **Reach:** a crossing between the Abyss and the First Hell takes up to 6 days, so however its course changes, the Styx never strays far from the corridor between them. Demons reach farther systems through harbingers (see Demons).
  - **No steering:** the one place in the sea where will doesn't move you; the wind carries you (see The astral sea, Propulsion).
  - **Organic ships** can swim the Styx in either direction at normal speed, but only behind a mind shield; without one they get confused and wander out of the river.
  - **The Blood War's front line:** armies move along it, which keeps the war out of sight of anyone who avoids the river.
  - **Daemons are the ferrymen:** only daemons can reliably navigate the Styx, so devils and demons who can't wait for the wind to turn must hire them.
- **Daemons** (D&D's yugoloths): neutral evil, mercenary fiends who sell their services to both sides of the Blood War.
- **Devils** (the Nine Hells): lawful evil, Klingon-like. They are defending themselves against the demons, and need to recruit souls or hire mercenaries to do so.
  - **Structure:** nine Hells, nested. Only the First Hell touches the astral sea; each deeper Hell can be reached only from the one above it. The devils set the First Hell's laws to hide the deeper Hells from the astral sea.
  - **Plane shifting** straight into a deeper Hell does work, but for any being to facilitate it is treason.
  - **Rank by depth:** each Hell is ruled by an archdevil, ranked from the First (nearest the astral sea, the Blood War's front line) down to the Ninth, where the supreme ruler sits.
  - **Contracts, not assimilation:** unlike the Abyss, the Hells never absorb planes. They bind them with treaties, protectorates and vassal contracts, so the empire is the nine Hells plus an outer ring of contracted worlds. Because the order is rigid, ambitious devils look for advantages outside it.

## Known planar systems

- **The Abyss:** the demons' system; each assimilated plane is one of its layers (see Fiends, Demons).
- **The Nine Hells:** the devils' nested Hells, only the First touching the astral sea (see Fiends, Devils).
- **Rosste:** a sandbox system, where the Astral Federation was founded (see The Astral Federation and Fleet).
- **Toril:** a sandbox system, the Forgotten Realms' world.
- **Greyhawk:** a sandbox system, the world of Oerth.
