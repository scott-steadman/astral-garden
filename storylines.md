# Astral Garden Storylines

How stories in the Astral Garden are written: guidance for every storyline, open questions, story hooks, and the index of formats and storylines. World facts live in `bible.md`; each storyline's own notes live in its `storyline.md` file.

## Guidance for every storyline

- Theme: **the role of reason in a world of magic.** Problems are solved by someone understanding how a thing works, not only by hitting harder.
- Craft: every scene advances the story and is interesting on its own; add sounds and smells, especially in character introductions; long sentences slow pace, short ones speed it; flashbacks and introspection slow pace.
- Point of view: each storyline (and format) chooses its own, recorded in its `storyline.md` file.
- Federation knowledge: how much of the bible the Federation knows (the Blood War, for example) is set per storyline, in its `storyline.md` file.
- Ships switching vessels: nothing prevents a ship's soul from switching between a phylactery and reincarnation, so further down the timeline there may be ships that have switched, one way or the other (bible: Ships, Ensouled ships).
- Ships talking to each other: stories can include scenes of ensouled and organic ships talking among themselves about what they've discovered, for example over a conference call. Such a scene makes a good cold open.
- Terminology: say "whole soul", never "un-sundered". Keep the three kinds of instant travel distinct: *shifting*, *teleporting* and *portaling* (bible: Instant travel).
- Keep it loosely based on D&D 5e.
- Try to make it playable, but story comes first. Stories may later be adapted into D&D adventures or campaigns, and adapting a great story beats forcing a story into a playable shape. Never let playability restrict the story.

## Open questions

World questions deliberately left for a story to answer. When a story settles one, record the answer in `bible.md`.

- How mortals ascend to godhood (bible: Gods, Ascension).
- How a whole soul becomes sundered (bible: Souls, Whole and sundered souls).
- How true names are discovered, and how long their control lasts and what limits it (bible: Souls, True names).
- Where Arcana comes from, and how it is generated (bible: Magic).
- What happens to a god when the Abyss assimilates its home plane.
- Why there are only nine Hells (a religious reason, a limit on the devils' power, or something else).
- Whether the Styx strips memories.
- See also the hooks below.

## Formats and storylines

There are five **formats**. Each story series within a format is a **storyline** with its own name and its own `storyline.md` file.

### Short form

A self-contained story, **up to an hour's read** (at most about 15,000 words). Each storyline can set its own target within that.

### Episodic

A series of short form stories (episodes), each up to an hour's read. Every episode tells one or more plots, and arcs can run across several episodes; each storyline records its arcs in its `storyline.md` file.

- **Astral Trek**: Star Trek style. `storylines/astral-trek/storyline.md`. Status: Episode 1 prompt ready (`storylines/astral-trek/prompt.md`).

### Long form

A novella or novel: one arc told in chapters, with room for deeper characters and slower reveals.

### Adventure

A playable D&D 5e module of one to three sessions, usually adapted from a favorite story (for example, an Astral Trek episode). A natural default shape: an adventure hook, locations with keyed areas, encounters with stat references, NPC motives, and a five-room-dungeon structure. Try to keep it playable, but story comes first.

### Campaign

A long playable D&D 5e arc that strings adventures together around a central threat.

## Hooks

### A totem market

Someone may run a market in planar totems (who, and whether it exists at all, is undecided). Saved for a storyline.

### The devil's secret farm

A devil secretly created a small flat-earth plane where it harvests the souls of captured races. No other deity knows the plane exists, so there is no divine magic there except a warlock's (power from the devil) or a divine soul sorcerer's (power from their own soul). Nothing in infernal law forbids it; the devil keeps it **secret** so rivals can't copy the trick or claim the souls. Could be long form or a campaign. To be fleshed out later.

### Killing a god

A plot to kill a god for good: first destroy its home plane, leaving it homeless, then kill it anywhere. Who wants it dead, and how do they get to its home plane? Saved for a storyline.

### The quiet circle

Under the Federation's Starfleet surface, something deeper may grow. Eventually a small, informal group of older, wiser beings forms, quietly trying to learn to shape outcomes. Mostly ships: at least one of the three founding organics and a handful of ensouled ships whose souls lived long before joining. Plus a few long-lived creatures outside the Fleet, such as an ancient dragon who advised the Federation's founders. They are learning, not masters: they nudge, sometimes get it wrong, and argue among themselves about how far they should go. Working name the quiet circle (members don't use a formal name). They meet on private conference calls. Its existence is a closely guarded secret. Whether it exists in a storyline, and whether crews or other ships know about it, is set in that storyline's `storyline.md` file.

### The avatar debate

Avatars are not available when the Federation is founded. They arrive only after a long debate over the morality of growing empty "biological vessels": is a body never conceived a person-in-waiting; does growing one take a home from a soul that might have been born into it; would the gods be offended; and do evil ships' ancillaries demand parity, or show where it leads? Until then, ships use drones. The debate itself is story material.

### Why anchors fail on the Hells

Abyssal anchors don't work on the Hells (bible: Fiends, Demons), and nobody outside the Hells knows why. Possible answers: the devils wrote it into the First Hell's laws; the Hells' barrier is powered by something an anchor can't grip; or an old contract with someone more powerful protects them. Whoever learns the truth could hand the demons a way in, or give other planes the same protection.

### Breaking the Abyss

A storyline about **destroying or detaching layers of the Abyss**. Perhaps the **quiet circle** decides to do it in secret, to keep the Abyss from growing (and from nearing 666) and to keep demons and devils busy fighting each other instead of expanding into other worlds. Questions for the story: how a layer is cut loose, what becomes of a freed plane and the souls on it, the moral cost of deliberately prolonging the Blood War, and what happens if the demons, the devils or the Federation find out who is doing it.

### The Styx comes through

The River Styx wanders, and for a while its course sweeps through an innocent planar system, carrying the **Blood War** right through it. Armies of devils and demons pass by, or stop, and the locals (or a Federation ship that happens to be there) are caught in the middle until the wind moves on.

### The soul in the drone

A character idea: a soul that didn't want to become a ship, but wanted to keep journeying with someone, so it chose to live in a phylactery attuned to a drone instead. That someone is a ship or a crewmate, and the soul could be their **friend, lover or spouse**, or an **enemy** who came along to taunt them. It travels as the drone, small and overlooked by everyone else. A **curse** may be involved: the soul, or the person it follows, may be bound to the other against their will.
