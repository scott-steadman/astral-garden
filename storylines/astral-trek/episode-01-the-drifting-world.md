# Astral Trek
## Episode One: "The Drifting World"

---

### Teaser

> **Captain's Log, 1.693.** It has been several weeks since we last sighted a planar system. The crew continues to perform well in readiness drills, and I see no fraying of order or discipline. Months confined aboard a ship on the astral sea turn out to be little different from months on an ocean, except that there is no weather and the accommodations are better. On the matter of Ship's own discipline, I have asked him to keep his bawdier repertoire confined to his tavern.

Captain Elyerith Indimel signed the entry, closed the logbook, and laid it in the compartment in her armrest. The lid shut with a soft thump. In a few minutes its twin at Fleet headquarters, a whole sea away, would fill with the same words in the same hand.

She was alone on the bridge, which was usual. The *Tranelis Selevarun* was an ensouled vessel. The soul of the ship did the work that crews of lesser vessels did with ropes and rosters, and a captain could speak to him from her bath if she wanted. Elyerith did not want. Captains belonged on bridges. The stations ringing the chamber would fill when there was something to coordinate, and the scryer at its center would bloom with drone-sight and maps. This morning it was dark, and the only company was the music.

She turned her chair a few degrees left, then right. It squeaked. *I need to get someone to fix that.*

The music was robust, all drums and brass, and she noticed her fingers tapping the armrest in time with it. Most ensouled ships spoke to their crews mind to mind. Tranelis Selevarun had been a sea-elf bard before he was a ship, and he had never seen any reason to stop using his voice.

"Ship?"

"Yes? Oh Captain, my Captain?"

"What is this piece called?"

"*Orlalassar's Battle Theme.*" She could hear the grin. Nothing pleased him more than someone asking about his music. "I used to play it whenever he went into battle. Which was often."

"I think I prefer it to *Oribella's Theme*," she said. *Oribella's* had a thread of tragedy running under it. This was all forward motion.

The music dropped away sharply.

"Captain, there's a system five minutes ahead. A prime material system. It's... abnormal."

"Full stop." She was already out of her chair. *Abnormal is rarely a good word.* "Abnormal how? Put it on the scryer."

The scryer lit. A planar system hung in the middle of the bridge: the prime material plane, a dark sphere, and near it the two smaller lights of the demi-planes where its gods lived. Gods liked to keep house close to the people they had made.

"Two demi-planes," Ship said. One of them brightened and a clear tone sounded. "This one looks normal. Lit from within, the way they should be." The other brightened, or tried to, and the tone it made was flat and short. It stayed dark. "This one is unlike anything we've seen. And then..." He paused, because he had been a performer all his short life and could not help it. An arrow appeared, leading from the prime plane out into the open astral. "The prime material plane is moving. Away from its gods. Slowly, roughly in that direction. I'll watch it and narrow down the heading."

Elyerith stood with her arms folded and looked at the arrow for a long moment. Planes drifted, as everything in the astral drifted. They did not leave their gods.

"Any threats nearby?"

"None that I can see, Captain."

"Take us in. Send drones to the demi-planes, the dark one first. Tell my officers to be ready to meet in council chambers as soon as we have preliminary results." She hesitated. "And have someone bring my breakfast up. I'll eat on the bridge."

*So much for stretching my legs.*

"Yes, Captain."

### Act One

Two decks down, in a workshop that smelled of lamp oil and sawdust and hot brass, Crewgnome Arili Savore was hunched over the tail end of a drone.

She had been hunched for two hours, and her back had started to tell her so, which meant it was nearly breakfast. Gnome eyes were good for this sort of work; she seldom needed a magnifier. A multi-tool in each hand, she seated the last pin of a small hinged door in the drone's rear casing, then opened and closed it a few times for the pure pleasure of the latch. *Click.* *Click.* Lovely.

She straightened and stretched, and her spine made a noise like a ratchet.

Kono floated in through the open door, gossamer wings beating slow. Faerie dragons flew by magic, not by air, so the flapping was for show, and Kono was a creature of show. He was carrying something limp and furry in his forelegs.

"What's up, gnomie?" He landed on her left shoulder. Her right was already taken. Manus, her spectral hand, was perched there in its usual gray glove, drumming its fingers.

"Just finishing the new drone."

Kono took a bite of the rodent. It crunched wetly, very close to her ear. "Looks nice. New case?"

"New case. With the old ones you had to take half the thing apart to swap the rod. This one, you pop the door and pull. If we're ever in a hurry, I want it to take a second, not a minute."

"What rod's going in?"

Arili answered with a question of her own. "Manus, would you bring me a Mark Five, please?"

"Ooh," said Kono.

Manus lifted off her shoulder and bounced across the workshop through the air, twiddling its fingers as it went, to a cabinet labeled *M-5 RODS*. It opened the door with a flourish, drew out a wooden rod etched with silver runes and capped at one end by a translucent bead, and shut the cabinet by pushing it with the rod. Then it floated back conducting an invisible orchestra. Arili held the little door open and nodded at it. With a final flourish, Manus slid the rod home. *Click.*

"Ship?"

"Yes, Arili?"

"Your new drone is ready."

"Oooh! A new toy. Thank you!"

The drone chimed its activation tones and rose off the bench. Ship spun it, tilted it, sent it sweeping along the shelves to test its senses. Manus took off after it, fingers spread to grab, and the drone slipped sideways out of reach every single time. Kono crunched. When Ship had finished playing tag, the drone blinked out of the workshop to take up its duties outside the hull.

"So." Kono licked a claw. "Yesterday you were about to tell me why you joined the Fleet."

"Oh, right. Same reason most young gnomes do. I was bored." She began putting tools away, each in its painted outline on the pegboard. "Every corner of my home plane was mapped. I wanted to go where no gnome had gone before. Then one day there were Fleet recruiters in the market square, and the first thing I noticed was their uniforms. Matching uniforms! Standing out like that borders on rude, back home. So naturally I went over."

"Naturally."

"I asked them what planes they'd been to, and they said none yet. The Fleet was three months old. Only a handful of ships were out. *But*, they said, if I signed up I could read every log those first ships sent back while I was in training, and by the time I graduated the next generation of ships would be launching and I could be on one. So I asked about the workshops and the magic for another hour and signed that afternoon." She shrugged. "I tested well. Did well. Got assigned here. I should make ensign soon."

"How's the studying?"

"Almost ready for the exam."

"I look forward to calling you Ensign Savore." Kono tossed the last of the rodent into his mouth. *Crunch.*

"I'm going to get some breakfast. I know you just ate, but you can come."

"Oh, I haven't had breakfast." Kono patted his belly and let out a small, dignified burp. "That was an appetizer. Something I picked up on the way here."

---

> **Captain's Log, 1.694.** The dark demi-plane belonged to a god called Maanzecorian. Chief Religionist Beiwaren is certain of the identification. Maanzecorian is dead, and its plane has been ransacked.
>
> **Supplemental.** The second demi-plane, the lit one, belongs to a god called Ilsensine. It too is vacant and ravaged. Ilsensine is nowhere to be found. Beiwaren notes that a god's demi-plane goes dark only when the god dies, so Ilsensine is presumably alive somewhere. I have to wonder whether it fled.
>
> **Captain's Log, 1.695.** Drones report that the prime material plane is a sunless world inhabited by a people who call themselves illithids. They live in a single great city built over a vast network of caverns, which they share with the troglodytes they raise as livestock. They are ruled by an entity called the Elder Brain. Given that their world is being carried off toward an unknown destination, I have decided not to delay first contact for further study.
>
> **Captain's Log, 1.696.** First contact is made. The Elder Brain knows that other planes exist, but its people have never sailed the astral. It did not know its world was moving. It speculated that this was connected to its recent loss of contact with one of its gods, which tells me it knew something was wrong and had said nothing to its people. I told it what we found on the demi-planes. It has invited a delegation to its city. I have accepted, cautiously. Every member of the delegation will wear a mind-shield.

---

The common chamber of the illithid city was black as the inside of a closed fist, until a point of light appeared in the middle of the air.

It hissed. It stretched, slowly, into a line, then with a lower hiss into a rectangle, and then, gathering speed as if it had decided to stop teasing, it resolved into a tall ornate door that glowed softly gold. Ship liked to make an entrance. A seam split the door down its center and it swung open without a sound.

Captain Indimel stepped through first. Grit crunched under her boots. The air was cool and close, and as her eyes adjusted she saw that the chamber was large and round, and that the beings waiting at its center were floating a hand's breadth off the floor, their robes hanging perfectly still.

Her nostrils flared. A paladin's gift came to her as smell. Good was clean, like air after rain, and evil was sulphur. Here there was both, braided together so tightly she could not have said which came from whom.

"Greetings. I am Captain Indimel of the *Tranelis Selevarun*. These are my officers. Chief Religionist Beiwaren."

Ena Beiwaren stepped forward, laid her hand on her chest below the pendant of her god, and bowed. The chain clinked as the pendant swung free.

"Chief Arcanist Ianthe."

Ellie Ianthe nodded once, the silver embroidery on her gold circlet faintly luminous in the gloom.

"And Chief Tinkerer Nightglow."

Hevin Nightglow bowed from where she stood. Her leather tool harness creaked. Not one tool shifted in its loop.

The drone floated out last, the door folded shut behind it, and the light went with it, leaving only the drone's soft glow and the faint phosphorescence of the walls.

*Greetings, Captain.*

The voice did not arrive through her ears. It pressed on the inside of her skull like a thumb on a bruise, and the mind-shield in her earpiece warmed in answer. It was huge, that voice, patient and cool, and it came from everywhere at once.

*These are members of my Elder Concord. Nourisher Saardsult. Minder Quoor. Creator Tharcerelin.*

Three of the floating figures drifted forward in turn, and Elyerith bowed to each. They were tall and lean, purple-hided, with domed heads and pale eyes and a fringe of tentacles that hung over their mouths and stirred as if in a current. They wore identical robes. To her eye, they were identical.

*They have names and titles. There must be some way to tell them apart.*

*So, Captain. You have not learned what moves our world, or where it is going?*

"Not yet. We're still investigating."

*We appreciate the effort. Ask for whatever you require.*

First contact took a little over an hour, and it was mostly the illithids asking questions. What other peoples were there? What was the astral sea like? How many planes? They claimed to have had no idea that there were other thinking beings at all. They listened to her offers of trade, and of membership in the Federation, and declined both quickly and courteously, and Elyerith let it go. A first conversation was not the time to press.

When at last the questions ran out, the Elder Brain asked, *Would you prefer to tour our city together, or separately?*

"Separately, please," Elyerith said. "We have such varied interests."

---

Her escort was Minder Quoor. Elyerith had already decided she liked it, which she did not entirely trust. It asked questions faster than she could answer them, and it seemed to actually want the answers.

"Is there somewhere in particular you would like to see, Captain?" it asked aloud, for her benefit. Its voice was dry and soft, like pages turning.

"Your places of worship. I study religions. It's a hobby of mine."

"We have only two gods. Ilsensine and Maanzecorian." A small pause. "The Grotto of Sacred Thoughts is Ilsensine's. If you will take my hand, I can bring us there."

Its hand had three long claw-tipped fingers and a thumb, and it was cool and dry. Air whooshed in to fill the space where they had been.

The Grotto was a sealed cavern, and every surface in it had been carved. The carvings showed an illithid's shape grown vast: a great brain with tentacles streaming out from it like the roots of a tree. *Created in Ilsensine's image,* she thought.

The Prime Venerator of the Grotto, Serisk, received her with a small bow. Elyerith asked whether there was telepathic iconography as well as carved, and Serisk said, "Indeed there is. Follow me, and you may experience it yourself in the Hall of Whispers."

*That doesn't sound at all malevolent,* Elyerith thought. *Thank the arcanists for these mind-shields.*

It was not far. She slowed as she walked through the arch, and her mouth fell a little open.

The Hall of Whispers did not whisper. It shouted. Thoughts that were not hers came at her from the walls like surf, all of them saying one thing in a thousand voices: *Kneel. Belong. Obey. Ilsensine above, the Brain below, and you between, held, held, held.* The shield behind her ear went hot. She set her teeth and let the wave break on her and draw back, and break again, and draw back. The sulphur in here was so thick she could taste it.

And yet. Threaded through the hall, faint, she could still smell the clean air after rain. Not every mind that worshipped here was evil. *How do good beings make room for an evil god?* she wondered, and could not answer.

Their scripture, Serisk told her afterward, was not written down. It was kept in fluid. In its chamber it set a gray vial and a clear one on a glowing rune-cloth, dipped one long finger into the gray up to the first knuckle, and stirred it through the clear. Over the course of a minute the clear fluid clouded and turned gray. Its tentacles screwed on the lid as the vial floated before its face. Then the vial drifted to Elyerith, and her palm tingled when it landed. "If your magic can read it," Serisk said, "you will know us better than most outsiders ever have." It did not say *than any*. It had never met another outsider.

Then Quoor took her hand again and brought her to the Cenotaph of Maanzecorian.

It was quieter here. Illithids hovered in a loose ring around a statue of their second god, still as moths, and Elyerith, at the edge of their thoughts, caught pieces of a conversation running among them. *A test of faith.* *No. It has abandoned us.* *It will speak again. It has always spoken again.* *Why does the Brain say nothing?*

They did not know. The Elder Brain had told its own people nothing about a dead god in a dark sky.

*Not so hive-minded as we're led to believe.*

"You're troubled, Captain," Quoor said quietly.

She looked at it. That face was impossible to read, but it had been watching her rather than the statue. "Are you?"

Quoor's tentacles curled and uncurled. "Maanzecorian was the god of secrets," it said at last. "It always seemed to me a poor joke that it would keep this one."

When they returned to the common chamber the rest of the delegation was already waiting, gray-faced with fatigue. Quoor floated a little closer.

"Before you go, Captain, would it be possible for me to tour your ship?"

"Of course. When would suit you?"

"This time tomorrow?"

"Then I'll pick you up here."

She bowed and walked to the center of the chamber, turned to the drone, and said, "Knock knock."

"Who's there?" said the drone, in Ship's voice.

"Ima."

"Ima who?"

"I'm about done here." She was grinning.

The golden door bloomed out of the dark. Elyerith waved her officers through and followed. On the other side, in the clean light of Ship's corridors, all four of them sagged at once.

"I did not know," said Ellie Ianthe, rubbing her temples, "that talking could be so exhausting."

Nobody disagreed.

---

### Act Two

*Decades earlier.*

Nourisher Saardsult had drifted beyond the reach of the Elder Brain only to be alone for an hour, which was a thing illithids were not supposed to want. So when the whisper came, there was no greater mind to ask what it was.

*What is this?* It turned slowly in the dark of the deep caverns. The whisper was not alive. It knew the texture of living thought, and this was something else, thin and steady, like a draft through a crack.

There. It moved toward it, slowly.

*I see you,* Saardsult sent. *What are you?*

*A channel,* the whisper answered.

---

Very far away, in a place that smelled of rot and furnaces, a demon called Zozzoluz knelt before its prince.

The Prince of the Undead was vast, bloated, and bored. Zozzoluz was gaunt and gray and over seven feet tall, with a horn that curved from the back of its skull over the top of its head and ended in a spike, and it was ambitious in the way all minor demons were: completely, and without the least idea what it would do once it got there.

"My prince. I have acquired an illithid."

"Have you." The prince did not look at it.

"I had never heard of them. I hoped you might tell me their vices, so I can use it."

"Illithids." The prince drew the word out, and something shifted behind its eyes as it reached across the planes to look. "Spawn of Ilsensine and Maanzecorian." A growl came up from deep in its chest. "I have unfinished business with Maanzecorian."

Zozzoluz knew how the planes worked. Gods kept their demi-planes close to the worlds they made. A world and its gods together were a planar system, and planar systems were scattered across the astral sea like islands. To reach one you had never visited, you had to sail there, or be summoned by someone already there.

And it had someone already there.

"If I bring you to that system," Zozzoluz said, "will you elevate me?"

"I will elevate you," said the prince, "when you have done two things. Bring me to that system. And deliver its prime material plane to the Abyss."

Elevations were easy to give and easy to take away. Keeping one was the recipient's problem. Zozzoluz did not think of that.

"Deal!"

*And before I deliver it,* it thought, *I'll see what I can carry off.*

"Now," said the prince. "You asked about illithid vices."

---

For decades Saardsult and its secret friend talked across the planes, and in those decades a lowly nourisher rose. A trick of the mind here, a word in the right ear there, and it climbed rank by rank until it floated among the Elder Concord itself.

"I have risen as high as my kind can rise," it told Zozzoluz at last. Confessing this to another illithid would have been death. But Zozzoluz was not an illithid, and it had never once failed it. "I am not content. I do not want to serve. I want to rule. And the Elder Brain is far stronger than I am."

This was the moment the demon had been patient for.

"I can help you destroy it," Zozzoluz said. "But I would have to call in favors from my kind, and they will want payment."

They haggled for months. In the end the terms were these. Zozzoluz would teach Saardsult a ritual to summon it. Zozzoluz and its friends would help Saardsult remove the Elder Brain. And when Saardsult ruled Illithus, the demons would receive six hundred head of troglodyte cattle, sixty tadpoles, and six treasures of their choosing from its vaults.

"Those are the terms?"

"Those are the terms."

"I will think on it."

"As you should, my friend."

It thought on it for a few cycles. It said yes.

---

*Now.*

The caverns where Zozzoluz kept its lair were warm, and far outside the Elder Brain's range, and Saardsult breathed in the heat with something like pleasure. The off-worlders had left the city two hours ago. It could still taste them in its memory: soft, bright, delicious minds, every one of them sealed shut.

"What is this about our world moving?" it asked, when it had told the demon everything. "Has it to do with that device you brought?"

Behind Zozzoluz, against the far wall of the chamber, the device sat humming. It was a squat thing of black iron and dull red stones, and the rock around it was cracked.

"It's nothing," said Zozzoluz, with no effort at all. "Every plane drifts. It's a trick, to frighten you into joining their federation. I told you, the anchor brought my friends here. I keep it in case we need more." A pause. "Are these visitors a threat to your plans?"

"They are. The ever-curious Quoor is touring their ship tomorrow. I would like to use that, if I can do it without exposing myself."

The demon's lipless mouth widened over its teeth. "Bring Quoor to me," it said, "and I'll make it do whatever you like."

---

The next day Minder Quoor waited in the common chamber with two companions.

"Just the three of you?" Elyerith asked.

"I am afraid the others are not adventurous. I invited friends instead. I hope you don't mind."

"Not at all. Please." She stood aside from the golden door.

On the far side, in Ship's corridor, Quoor made a small fluttering motion with its fingers. "Oh, where are my manners. These are Dakt and Gassk."

They were illithid-shaped, in illithid robes. They floated. Elyerith nodded to each of them in turn, and breathed in, and did not let her face change.

Sulphur. Sulphur so strong it was like walking past a forge. From all three of them, and from Dakt and Gassk most of all.

"Welcome aboard the *Tranelis Selevarun*. These are my senior officers, some of whom you've met. Commander Norralei. And Security Chief Irla."

Kysxaris Norralei inclined her head, her half-elven face as serene as a still pond. Irla, broad as a door and wearing his uniform a size too tight on purpose, bowed slightly from the neck. Neither of them looked at the captain. Both of them had heard what she had not said. She had not introduced Ship.

---

"Crewgnome Savore," said Ship.

"Mm?" Arili didn't look up from her bench.

"The captain would like you to come up to the bridge and fix the squeak in her chair."

"*Now?*"

"Now."

She blinked. "On my way."

"I'll let the commander know."

Tools clinked as she packed them. Manus settled onto her shoulder, and she checked the oil can twice, because if the captain wanted the chair fixed right this minute she was not going to show up without oil. The toolbox latches snapped shut.

---

When the captain brought her guests out of the ready room, the bridge was fully staffed for the first time in weeks. Every station was manned. The scryer was alight with a slowly turning map of Illithus. And in the middle of it all, a gnome in gray coveralls was spinning slowly around in the captain's chair, listening to it not squeak.

The captain's eyes slid to her for half a heartbeat. The corner of her mouth went up.

"Captain on the bridge!" Norralei called.

Arili hopped down and came to attention so fast she nearly dropped the oil can.

"As you were. I'm giving our guests a brief look at the bridge, and then we'll be on our way." The captain led them toward the center. "The ship is heavily automated. These stations around the edge are where the bridge officers and senior staff sit on duty, facing the center so everyone can see the scryer. That's how we speak with our drones, and with anyone outside the ship."

"Ah, yes," said Quoor. "Very nice." It sounded far away. Its tentacles had gone completely still.

Dakt and Gassk drew daggers from their sleeves and screamed.

It was not an illithid sound. It was something else, something with teeth in it, and they flung themselves at the nearest crew.

"Zozzoluz," Quoor said, in a voice that was not quite its own, "the time has come." It lifted its staff and drew it slowly downward through the air.

The air tore.

A ragged, glowing wound opened along the path of the staff, and hot wind blew through it onto the bridge, stinking of rot and furnaces. Behind it something groaned, low and enormous. As the tear widened Arili saw a face on the other side, gray and lipless, pressing toward the gap, and behind that face more faces, and behind those more, packed in the dark like fish in a net.

The bridge crew were on Dakt and Gassk. The captain was running for Quoor, and she was too far away. Arili could see it, the geometry of it, the captain's stride against the widening of that tear. If it opened all the way, they would pour through, and they would kill everyone, and then they would have the ship.

Arili was closest.

"*Manus!*" She punched the air with her left fist.

Manus launched off her shoulder like a stone from a sling. In mid-flight it folded itself into a fist, and it drove straight into Quoor's face.

Quoor's head snapped back. Its concentration broke. The tear guttered. The demon on the other side lunged with one long arm, and the wound in the air slammed shut on it with a sound like a door in a gale. Something gray and clawed dropped to the deck, twitched, and began to smoke.

The captain was still coming. She raised both hands over her head, clasped as if around a hilt, and cried, "*Telantes!*" There was a flash as she brought them down, and a sword was in them, blazing, on its way to Quoor's skull.

Quoor got the staff up just in time. *Crack.*

Arili had no weapon. She had an oil can. She dropped it, tumbled forward under Quoor's elbow, grabbed the staff with both hands, and hung on. Quoor snarled, let go of it, whipped a curved dagger from its robe, and spat a word. A shimmering shield sprang up, and the captain's next thrust sheared off it in a shower of sparks.

So Arili hit it with its own staff.

Steel rang on steel, and the captain and Quoor wheeled around the scryer while the map of Illithus turned serenely between them. Arili jabbed at Quoor's back and knees. Manus seized a fistful of its face-tentacles and pulled. Behind them Irla roared and something went over with a crash, and in less than a minute Dakt and Gassk were down. Quoor, alone, could not cover three directions at once. The captain drove it back, and back, until it stumbled and flung its arms wide for balance and left its chest open.

The captain's blade went through it, and the wound lit up gold.

Quoor's death-cry was not a sound. It went through every mind on the bridge at once, a single bright note of anguish, and stopped. The minder was dead before it reached the deck.

The doors burst open on a squad of security officers with weapons out.

"What happened?"

"Our guests tried to take the ship," said the captain.

Where Dakt and Gassk had fallen there were now only two spreading puddles of something gray and viscous, with illithid robes lying empty in them. Demons in borrowed shapes. Only Quoor's body was real. The captain let her sword go. It vanished with a soft pop, and the blood that had coated it pattered down onto the corpse. There was blood on her hands, too, and on her sleeves. *Time for a shower.*

"Take the minder's body to Chief Religionist Beiwaren. I want to know what it knew. Ship?"

"Yes, Captain?"

"Send someone up to clean this ectoplasm."

"Yes, Captain." Ship's voice was very quiet. He had not played a note since Quoor raised the staff.

"Well played, Captain," said Norralei.

"I thought the bridge was where they'd try it. It's where it *looks* like the ship is run from."

"What tipped you?"

The captain touched the side of her nose. "They smelled evil. Dakt and Gassk especially."

"Irla and I went on alert when you didn't introduce Ship."

"Well done, Commander." The captain turned. "Crewgnome Savore!"

Arili, who had been quietly trying to become invisible behind the captain's chair, snapped to attention. "Yes, Captain!"

"Why do you think I called you up here?"

Arili opened her mouth. Closed it. "To... fix the chair, Captain?"

"I needed the bridge to look ordinary. Nothing looks more ordinary than somebody fixing a chair." The captain looked at her for a moment, and there was something approving in it. "I didn't expect you to save it. Your quick thinking turned this fight in the first three seconds. If that pack had come through, we'd be counting our dead."

"Manus and I are glad we could help, Captain." On her shoulder, Manus touched two fingers to her forehead in salute.

"I need people who think like that on my away teams. As it happens, I have an opening. Interested?"

"Yes, Captain!"

"Good. Norralei."

"Captain?" Norralei was grinning.

"See that *Acting Ensign* Savore is assigned to an away team."

### Act Three

In the warm dark beneath Illithus, the gateway had collapsed in Zozzoluz's face and taken most of one of its soldiers' arms with it.

The demon stood very still for a moment. Then it growled, a long, low, grinding sound, and turned to the pack crowded behind it.

"Fine," it said. "We do it the old way. Chaos and blood." It pointed two clawed fingers at the largest of them. "You two. Guard the manor door. Nothing gets in. The rest of you," and it grinned, "go into the city and enjoy yourselves. I'm going to summon more."

---

"Acting Ensign Arili Savore, reporting as ordered, ma'am."

Lieutenant Kimberly Anjel leaned back in her chair, her amaranthine skin dark against the pale bulkhead, and flashed a grin. "At ease, Savore."

Arili knew her from the gymnasium, where the lieutenant seemed to live. They had sparred a dozen times. Kimberly fought better than Arili did and liked to try odd things at close quarters, and Arili, who had never thought she would enjoy fighting, had found out that she loved it.

Kimberly hooked a foot around the leg of the chair on the far side of the desk and kicked it out. It scraped across the floor and stopped exactly in front of Arili.

"Have a seat. I'll tell you my goals, my methods, and what I expect. Then you can ask me anything. Then you'll meet the team."

"Yes, ma'am."

"My goal is to lead the best away team in the Fleet. That means we train hard, and more importantly, we train smart. You're a tinkerer?"

"Yes, ma'am!"

"Good. Never had one. I want to see what you bring. Tomorrow morning I want your readiness regimen on my desk, and I want a couple of hours a day in it for tinkering."

*A couple of hours a day.* Arili tried not to look like she was about to float.

"One more thing. The reason I asked for you is that you gave me good feedback when we sparred. Specific. Useful. You told me when I dropped my guard, which most people won't do to an officer." Kimberly leaned forward. "I want that. Candid feedback is how a team gets better. If you think I'm wrong, you say so, to my face. Understood?"

That surprised Arili more than anything else. Most officers she knew thought their way was the only way. "Understood, ma'am."

An hour later Kimberly led her into the team barracks and announced, "All right, Devils!"

*Well, that answers that.* The rest of the ship called Away Team One *Anjel's Devils*, and Arili had wondered whether the lieutenant knew.

Ensign Arastruna Greatbrand was a dwarf with a healer's insignia stitched bold on her shoulder and a handshake that could crack walnuts. Ensign Hiltrude Undertree, the team's scout, was a halfling not quite as tall as Arastruna; Arili recognized her from the ship's grove, where she practiced archery with the Arch-druid. And Ensign Zanji Miggledy ("wizard extraordinaire," said Kimberly, with a look) was a gnome like Arili, with the top third of a filigreed wand sticking out of a pocket sewn into her uniform, and the bright, slightly dangerous expression of someone who was always about to explain something.

"You're the one who punched the minder," Zanji said.

"Manus punched the minder."

Manus waved.

"I like her," said Zanji.

---

Chief Religionist Beiwaren had mixed feelings about speaking with the dead.

When a death was sudden, it could be a mercy. The dead could leave last words for the people who loved them, or pass on a secret they should have told while living. An interrogation was another matter. The dead were under no obligation to tell the truth.

*At least I don't have to resort to anything worse,* she thought, as the bearers laid Quoor's body on the dais in her shrine and withdrew to the door. She had never spoken with the dead of a telepathic race. *This should be interesting.*

She lit incense in a censer, waved it over the corpse, and let the smoke settle across the gray-purple face as she began to pray. She set the censer at its head and took her holy symbol in her fist. As the last word left her lips, her mind filled with fury.

*Saardsult led me into a trap! I will make it wish it had stayed a tadpole in the pool!*

"I may be able to help with that," Beiwaren said gently.

*Who are you?*

"Chief Religionist Beiwaren, of the *Tranelis Selevarun*."

A pause. Then the fury drained away, and what was left only sounded tired. *Ah. Tell your captain I am sorry. I was not myself.*

"Tell me what happened."

*After your captain left, Saardsult asked to meet with me. To discuss your visit, it said. It chose a chamber deep in the old caverns. I did not notice until too late that we were beyond the Brain's hearing. Then the door closed, and something took my mind from me. I was brought through a portal into a great bare chamber. There was a demon, Zozzoluz, and others, and a device against the far wall, black iron and red stone. The rock around it was cracked. And the whole chamber,* the dead voice said, *was groaning. As if the world itself were in pain.*

Beiwaren wrote until the spirit had told her everything, and then a little longer, after it was gone.

> **Captain's Log, 1.698.** Minder Quoor's spirit confirms that it was mind-controlled by a demon named Zozzoluz, and that it was delivered to that demon by Nourisher Saardsult of the Elder Concord. From Quoor's description, Chief Arcanist Ianthe believes the demon's device is an Abyssal Anchor, which is ordinarily used to pull demons through from the Abyss. She suspects it is also what is dragging Illithus across the astral. If she is right, Illithus is being towed toward the Abyss itself, perhaps to be made part of it.
>

"Listen up!"

The briefing room was full and loud, and Norralei's voice cut it in half. Chairs thumped and squeaked as five away teams turned to face the map on the wall.

"Ship has found the source of the raids. Here." She tapped a point in the caverns. "A dwelling outside the Elder Brain's range. The demons use these passages," a sweep of her hand over a maze of tunnels, "to hit the capital from every side.

She turned, found Arili's team, and held their eyes.

"Team One. Your mission is to find the device. Capture it if you can, destroy it if you must. The dwelling is guarded by two demon brutes. We didn't risk scouting it, so we don't know what's inside. The religionists say the brutes give off a necrotic aura out to about thirty feet. It'll turn your stomach, and we don't know whether they can feel you entering it." Her finger slid to a side passage. "You and your drone shift in here, out of their line of sight. You go down the passage, you kill the guards, you go in. They're demons, so you won't have to bother hiding the bodies.

There were none.

The latest pack had just padded out of sight of the guards when Ship shifted Team One into the side passage with a soft whoosh of displaced air.

"Right," Kimberly breathed. "I take the left one. The rest of you, right. Got it?"

Nods. They edged down the passage, and thirty feet from the brutes the aura hit them all at once, like a mouthful of spoiled meat. Arili's stomach turned over. She swallowed hard. Kimberly looked into each of their faces, one by one, and then counted down on her fingers. *Three. Two. One.*

Zanji, Hiltrude, and Arastruna stepped out and opened fire on the right-hand brute. Kimberly and Arili charged the left.

Kimberly's legs were twice as long as Arili's, and she got there first. Arili ran straight at the right-hand brute, which was the feint, and at the last instant tumbled left, came up behind Kimberly's brute, and drove her sword into its back. It grunted, surprised, as the point burst out of its chest dripping ichor. She planted her boots on its back, kicked off, tore the blade free, and somersaulted toward the other one.

Manus had already gone for it. But the second brute didn't wait to be distracted. It bunched its legs and leapt fifteen feet straight at the archers, and Zanji screamed as its barbed tail sank into her shoulder.

Hiltrude dropped her bow, drew her short-sword in the same motion, and cut the tail off at the root. Kimberly crashed into the brute from behind and took it to the ground, hammering at it with her pommel. It threw her off and was halfway up when Arili landed and slashed both its hamstrings. It went down face-first onto the cobbles. Kimberly's sword came around in a short, wet arc, and its head rolled.

Zanji was gray. Boils were already rising around the barb in her shoulder. Hiltrude pulled the barb free, ripped her sleeve open, pressed two fingers to the wound, and spoke one hard word. The boils stopped spreading. The skin around them still looked like something left too long in the sun.

"I'm fine," Zanji said, through her teeth.

"Well fought," Kimberly said quietly. "Savore. Have a look inside. Rest of us, stack up left of the door."

Arili listened at the doorway for half a minute, put her head around it, and slipped in. A few minutes later she was back, signaling the all-clear, and she led them into a side bedroom out of sight of the main room.

"Wait here. I'll scout the rest."

"Hold on." Zanji fished in her component pouch and came out with a little translucent ball with an eyelash sealed inside. She whispered to it and swirled it at Arili, and Arili's hands disappeared, and then the rest of her.

"Oh! Thanks."

The dwelling had been a minder's home, once. It was a slaughterhouse now. In one room she found what was left of the servant, scattered and stinking of old blood. In the rear bedroom a portal shimmered on the far wall, and beside it an archway led into a closet. She had just stepped into the closet when the portal began to hum.

She flattened herself against the wall beside the arch. *I hope demons can't see through invisibility.*

Six demons came through. Arili watched as five of them each handed something small to the sixth. The sixth turned and went back through the portal. The other five padded out toward the city.

When they were gone, she ran.

"Keys," she said, back in the bedroom, breathless. "They each carry something, and the last one takes them all back. I think the portal only lets you through if you've got one."

"Then we ambush the next group, take their keys, and go through," said Kimberly. "If the anchor's anywhere, it's on the other side. If they keep to schedule, we have a couple of minutes."

The portal room was twenty feet square, too small for five to fight in. Zanji, Hiltrude, and Arastruna lined the wall outside the door. Kimberly took a spot fifteen feet from the portal. Arili crouched right beside it, scooped a handful of caltrops from her belt pouch, and scattered them across the floor between the lieutenant and the portal. They pattered like hail.

The portal brightened, and hummed.

Three came through: two brutes and the escort. All three stepped onto the caltrops with bare feet and yelped. Kimberly slid forward, her feet skating to stay clear of the spikes, and took the escort's head off before it knew where it was. That one, at least, would not be warning anybody.

Arili waited until both brutes were on Kimberly, then sprang from her crouch and went for the backs of their legs. One whirled on her. Its tail whipped at her again and again, and she ducked, and Manus slapped it aside, and the brute bellowed in frustration. From the doorway, bolts and arrows hissed past Arili's head into the brutes. It helped to be short. It helped that Kimberly had put her brute between herself and the door. It did not last long.

Kimberly crouched over the bodies and went through them. She held up her palm. Three small black stones, warm, faintly humming.

"Crap. Three keys. There are five of us."

"No worries," said Zanji. Her eyebrows were doing something enthusiastic. "This is just like the logic puzzles I studied at the academy. Three of us go through. One comes back with all three keys. Then that one brings the last two."

Kimberly looked at her. "That's the plan, Miggledy."

"It's a good plan."

"It's the only plan."

"It's still good."

### Act Four

Arili went first.

The portal was cold, like stepping through a sheet of falling water, and she came out crouched on stone in total darkness. Behind her the portal hummed. Her eyes adjusted fast: a natural cavern, one exit, and nobody in it. She had arrived unseen. Now to make sure everyone else did. She crept to the exit and took up a spot against the wall where she could see and hear down the tunnel.

Kimberly and Zanji came through together. Kimberly, who could not see in the dark, whispered a word, and her armor began to glow, very faintly.

"Undetected," she murmured. "Good." She turned to hand Zanji her key, and stopped.

Beside the portal, on the floor, lay a heap of small black stones. Dozens of them.

There was a pause.

"Grab enough for everybody," Kimberly said.

Zanji scooped up a handful, looking slightly betrayed, and went back.

When the rest of the team and the drone came through, the drone made a small, sad, falling sound and dimmed.

"What was that?"

"It's lost Ship," said Arili.

"Something on this side is blocking him," said Zanji. "Has to be the portal."

"We were going to destroy it anyway," said Kimberly. "Let's find out if you're right."

Zanji drew her wand, pointed it at the portal, and spoke three words Arili knew but couldn't have repeated. The hum faded. The portal folded in on itself with a small pop. The drone chirped, cheerful again.

"Good," said Kimberly. "Savore, take point. Rest of us, standard order, thirty feet back."

Arili crept along the wall. She had not gone far when she heard voices. She held up a hand behind her, tapped her ear, pointed ahead, and went on alone.

The tunnel opened into a cavern with five demons in it, and the smell hit her like a wall. She clamped her jaw on her gag. *Demons must not have a sense of smell.* They were using the place as a dining hall and a latrine at the same time. Unidentifiable carcasses swung on chains from the ceiling. Heaps of excrement dotted the floor, and there was no clean path from one side to the other. The demons stood around the hanging meat, talking in their skin-crawly language.

Arili backed away before she could be noticed.

"Cavern. Five demons, exit on the far side," she reported. "All the same kind. Probably the next raiding party. Brace yourselves. It stinks."

"Quick plan," whispered Kimberly. "We get as close as we can without being seen. Miggledy makes me and Savore invisible. We go in and set up. When you hear us start the party, you come around the corner and engage. Got it?"

"Aye," they breathed.

Hiltrude motioned them close. She drew out a sprig of something and twirled it, scattering a pinch of ash over each of their heads. "That'll cover our sound."

Just before the tunnel's last bend, Zanji brought out the eyelash-ball, murmured, and touched Arili and Kimberly with her wand. Just before Kimberly vanished, Arili caught hold of her belt so she could follow her in. *Good thing our combat uniforms aren't plate.*

They crept into the stink. Kimberly picked her mark and set herself behind it. She tapped the back of Arili's hand twice. Arili slid into place.

Kimberly said something rude, cheerfully, and brought her sword down into the demon's skull. Arili cut its legs out from under it. It didn't even scream. It fell and began to run to ichor.

The other four came apart. Three of them made a wet, squelching sound, folded into leathery bat-like shapes, and flung themselves at the two of them. The fourth turned and ran for the far exit.

*Drat. We should have taken that one first.*

Arili and Kimberly went after it with the bat-things clawing at their backs. Arrows and a crackle of Zanji's magic came from behind as the rest of the team rounded the corner, and the bat-things dropped, one, two, three. But the runner was gone down the tunnel, and they could hear it howling.

They rounded a bend, and there in the mouth of the next chamber stood Zozzoluz.

It was taller than Kimberly, gray and gaunt, its lipless mouth all gums and teeth. Its horn swept back from its skull and over and down. It saw them, raised both hands high over its head, spat something in that language that made Arili's skin try to crawl off her bones, and brought its hands down.

The ceiling came down with them. The last thing Arili heard was the roar of the tunnel falling in.

---

She woke to pain, and to the cool tingle of a healing patch against her neck.

"You were lucky," said Arastruna, leaning over her. Her healer's insignia was faintly lit. "Only part of the roof came down. You weren't crushed."

"Gnomes and dwarves," Arili croaked, getting her elbows under her, "have different definitions of *lucky*."

Together they dug Kimberly out of the rubble, along with a drone so mangled it looked as though someone had stepped on it. Arastruna pressed a patch to Kimberly's throat, and she coughed herself awake.

The tunnel ahead was solid stone from floor to ceiling.

"What's the plan, Lieutenant?" Zanji asked.

Kimberly sat back against the rock with her eyes closed. "Stalemate. They can't get out, and we can't get in. We rest. We think."

They chewed rations in silence. The drone lay in the dust like a dead bird. Ship was on the other side of that rock, and so was the anchor, and so was a whole tunnel network of demons that Teams Four and Five were fighting their way through, and with every minute the world was dragged a little farther toward the Abyss.

"The first thing I think of," Zanji said at last, "is that I can get a few of us into the next room with a spell. We'd surprise them. But it'd be two or three of us against however many they've got."

Arili was looking at the drone.

She had built that case so you could change the rod in a second. *If we're ever in a hurry.* And what did a drone's rod do? It held one charge. One trip, anywhere at all, for whatever stood inside its field. The drone carried it so Ship could whisk the drone from place to place. But the rod didn't care what it was carrying.

"Oh," she said. "Oh, I have an idea."

She crawled over to the wreck. The casing was crumpled, but the little door at the back still opened. *Click.* She reached in, drew out a wooden rod etched with silver runes, its bead still glowing, and held it up, grinning.

"Aha!"

"What's that?"

"The drone's travel rod. It's how it teleports from place to place, or shifts from plane to plane. It's still charged, and it doesn't care whether it's carrying a drone or five people standing close together. I can put all of us in that room."

Kimberly looked at the rod, and at Arili, and laughed out loud for the first time all day. "Well done, Savore. You just saved the mission."

Arastruna's insignia brightened as she began to pray over them. Half an hour later, Kimberly stood.

"Everyone ready? Form up on Savore. Anything we need to do?"

"Stand close. Touch me if you can." Arili raised the rod. "Ready? Three. Two. One."

As she triggered it she saw Zanji slip something out of her component pouch, and then, with a soft pop, the cavern was empty.

---

The chamber was dim and full of haze, and the first thing they felt was the groan.

It came from everywhere, from the floor and the walls and up through their bones, a deep constant grinding like some huge thing in pain. Every few seconds a sharp crack rang out as rock split somewhere under the strain. The air was hot and thick and greasy, as if they had materialized inside a frying pan, and it stank. Against the far wall the anchor sat humming, black iron and red stone, and the floor around it was crazed with cracks.

And the room was full of demons.

Arili counted, and stopped counting. They were outnumbered. She didn't know yet whether they were outmatched.

Zanji shouted a word. Thunder burst in the middle of the demon pack, and every eye in the room snapped to her. That was what she'd pulled from her pouch. She, Hiltrude, and Arastruna fell back toward the rubble. There was nothing to take cover behind. This was going to be a high-stakes tavern brawl.

Zozzoluz turned to face them. Up close it was worse: seven feet of gray hide and claws, cat-legged, tail spiked, a spear in its fist. It spoke, and the words went into their minds like hooks, and for one long second every one of them watched her own death. Zozzoluz seemed to swell, to double, to fill the room as it came for them in long, low strides.

*Kimberly will go for the boss,* Arili thought. *Somebody has to thin out the rest before they get organized.*

She ran at Zozzoluz right beside Kimberly, and the pack, howling, ran right past the two of them toward Zanji. At the last second Arili peeled away, turned, and fell in behind them. They never looked back. She cut one down from behind, then another. Zanji and Hiltrude circled along the walls, working on whatever came nearest. Every demon that came out of that fight wounded found Arili at its back. Soon they were not outnumbered anymore. Soon there was only Zozzoluz.

Arastruna began to chant. Whatever she was calling on, Zozzoluz hated it. It broke off from Kimberly, crossed the chamber in two bounds, and backhanded the dwarf so hard she flew. She was unconscious before she landed.

Kimberly reached it first. It slipped her stroke, turned, and locked eyes with her. It said something, and Arili's earpiece translated it in a flat little voice: *You cannot harm me, weakling.*

Kimberly's sword arm dipped. Only an inch. But Arili saw it, and saw in Kimberly's face that she felt it, the strength running out of her like water from a cracked jug.

So Kimberly dropped her shoulder and hit it with her whole body. She drove with her legs and shoved the demon back across the chamber, away from Arastruna, while it rained blows on her back and arms. Arili darted in at its flank, cutting, so that it had to use its tail to stay upright instead of to strike. Behind them, Hiltrude reached Arastruna and got her breathing.

Zanji made for the anchor.

She held a pearl against her temple and tapped the device with her wand, here, and here, and here, listening. "Ianthe was right!" she shouted over the groaning. "It's this! It's pulling the whole plane!"

Zozzoluz heard. It reversed its spear and slammed the butt down on the back of Kimberly's head, and the lieutenant dropped. It turned toward Zanji.

It turned its back on Arili.

She put her sword into it to the hilt.

Zozzoluz sank to its knees. It muttered something, low and fast and vicious, in that crawling tongue. Arili's earpiece clicked and said nothing. Then the demon pitched forward onto its face and began to run to ichor.

It had felt the blade go in, and it knew its time on this plane was over. It spent its last breath on a curse, and it aimed the curse at the friend who had failed it.

Arastruna limped over, knelt, and laid a hand on Kimberly's chest. Light poured from her insignia, and the lieutenant gasped awake.

At the anchor, Zanji put her pearl away and pressed a sequence of the red stones, one, two, four, three. The groaning shuddered, faltered, and stopped.

The silence was enormous. Into it, all their earpieces crackled at once.

"...tenant Anjel, this is Commander Norralei. Do you read?"

Kimberly was still flat on her back. "Commander. Anjel. Mission successful. Device secured. Minor casualties."

They heard the commander let out a long breath. "Well done, Lieutenant. Tell me when it's ready to move, and we'll open you a door."

---

> **Captain's Log, 1.699.** Away Team One has killed the demon Zozzoluz and captured the Abyssal Anchor intact. Illithus has stopped moving. The last of the raiding parties have been destroyed by Teams Two, Four, and Five, fighting alongside the illithid militia. It is time to settle the matter of Nourisher Saardsult.

The Elder Concord was assembled in the common chamber when Elyerith came through the golden door, with only the drone and Beiwaren and her notes behind her. The illithids floated in their ring, robed, identical.

Except that they weren't.

She breathed in, and this time she could tell. It wasn't the sulphur of evil, of which there was a little here and a little there. It was the rot-and-furnace stink of the Abyss that had blown through the tear on her bridge. It was faint, and it clung to only one of them, the way smoke clings to someone who has stood too long by a fire.

"Elder Brain," she said, "your world is no longer moving. The demon who moved it is dead. But it did not come here uninvited." She turned and looked straight at the third figure from the left. "Nourisher Saardsult. Quoor sends its regards."

For a heartbeat, nothing happened.

Then Saardsult screamed.

It was not because of her. Elyerith understood that later, when Beiwaren put what the away team had heard together with what Quoor's spirit had told her. A death-curse travels as fast as hate does, and Zozzoluz had had a great deal of hate. Saardsult's body arched in the air. Its head swelled and swelled, the dome of its skull stretching, its tentacles lengthening and thickening and lashing, and its robes split. It grew. It rose above the others, half again their height, and its mind blew outward through the chamber like a gale. Elyerith's mind-shield went white-hot behind her ear.

And the vast, cool presence of the Elder Brain, which had filled the room since she entered it, reached for Saardsult and found nothing there.

The Concord drew back. One of them made a sound like a child. The mind that had been Saardsult was no longer part of anything the Elder Brain could touch. It had been cut out of the whole, and it had become a whole of its own.

*Traitor,* said the Elder Brain, and for the first time Elyerith heard something other than patience in it. *Abomination. Begone from my world.*

Saardsult looked down at the captain. Its eyes were the same pale eyes. Everything else had changed.

*I wanted to rule,* it said. Its voice was huge now too. *Very well. I will rule elsewhere.*

There was a sound like many voices rising at once, from the corridors and the deep chambers of the city beyond, as illithids who had been waiting for its word suddenly heard it in their heads. Then Saardsult was gone, and so, she learned later, were forty-one others.

The chamber was silent.

*You knew, Captain,* the Elder Brain said at last. *Before you came.*

"Since yesterday."

*You did not tell me.*

"You didn't tell your people that Maanzecorian was dead," Elyerith said. "I think we both had our reasons."

The Elder Brain was quiet for a long time.

*We will trade with you,* it said finally. *We will not join you.*

"I thought you might say that." She bowed. "We'll leave the door open."

### Tag

"Attention on deck!"

Uniforms rustled and boots thumped as the whole crew of the *Tranelis Selevarun* came to attention in Ship's great hall. Arili stood with her team in her new dress uniform, chin up. On her shoulder Manus stood at attention too, in a glove dyed to match. Kono drifted near the rafters. Ship played a ceremonial march, quietly, the way he played when he was proud of everyone.

The captain walked down the center aisle and stopped behind the podium.

"At ease." She looked out at them. "Our mission to Illithus is complete. The demons are gone, and the plane is back where it belongs. It's my privilege to recognize some of the people responsible.

"Away Team One. For capturing the Abyssal Anchor and stopping the incursion at its source, the Distinguished Service Ribbon."

The hall erupted. Kimberly led them down the aisle one by one: Arastruna, still limping; Hiltrude; Zanji, the shoulder of her uniform padded over a bandage; and Arili last.

"Away Teams Two, Four, and Five, for driving the demons out of the capital alongside the illithid militia, the Service Ribbon."

More applause. More boots on the aisle.

"And lastly. Crewgnome Savore."

Arili's heart turned over. She went back up.

"Your quick action on the bridge stopped a demon invasion of this ship before it started. Your thinking after the cave-in saved your team's mission. You are promoted to Ensign, effective now, and permanently assigned to Away Team One." The captain pinned the insignia to Arili's collar herself. Arili saluted, and the captain returned the salute and then, unexpectedly, shook her hand.

"Thank you, Captain."

As she walked back, Kono swooped down from the rafters and hung in the air beside her, wings flickering. "Congratulations, *Ensign* Savore," he said. "Told you."

"Thanks, Kono."

"Take the rest of the day," the captain said. "Rest. Celebrate. Ship, play them something better than a march." The hall laughed. "Fleet headquarters has ordered us to find out where Illithus was being taken. We leave tomorrow."

She let that sit in the air a moment.

"Dismissed."
