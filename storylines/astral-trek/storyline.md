# Astral Trek: Storyline Setting

The episodic storyline in the Astral Garden. Shared world rules live in `../../bible.md`; this file holds what is specific to Astral Trek.

## Format

- Star Trek-style episodes. Each is self-contained, **up to an hour's read** (at most about 15,000 words; aim for 10,000 to 12,000), with a light series arc.
- Tone: serious stakes, warm crew, regular humor (the mascot, and Ship's sense of humor, perhaps witty repartee between them). Not grimdark.
- Timeline: the series begins in **year 1** of the Federation's calendar (stardates like 1.693; bible: Calendar). The Federation is young, so ensouled ships are rare and new. Ships use drones; there are no avatars yet.
- Structure: Episodes usually open with a captain's log to set things up, but that is a habit, not a rule; vary it when another opening works better. Logs can also bridge scenes and carry exposition.
- The captain's log: the captain dictates the entry to Ship, who may add a comment she didn't ask for. How logs are stored or reach the Fleet is up to the writer, if it's relevant.
- Crew logs: anyone aboard can dictate a log to Ship at any time. They're private unless the speaker or Fleet rules say otherwise, so Ship may hear things he can't repeat.
- Ship's own log: an occasional option, but not the episode's opening.
- Quiet circle (storyline-specific): doesn't exist yet.
- Characters' abilities should be recognizable as D&D 5e classes and spells, but never weaken a scene to keep it rules-accurate.
- Levels stay behind the scenes: never state a ship's or character's level in the story. Use level privately, when it's relevant to the story, to judge how powerful a being is and which 5e abilities they have.
- Feel free to riff on the premise or dilemma of an actual Star Trek episode, but don't retell the episode.

## What people know (year 1)

The bible is for the writer (see `storylines.md`). At the start of the series:

- **Known:** Federation operations (calendar, charts, *sending*, ranks, totems, shifting, teleporting and portaling), how magic works in practice, and that gods, devils and demons exist.
- **Disputed:** what happens after death, and whether a god can die for good.
- **Unknown:** whole and sundered souls, how true names work, harbingers, abyssal anchors, planar drift, the Blood War and the 666 belief.
- **Drift:** the Federation doesn't know planes drift. Its charts list fixed coordinates, without the drift figures in the bible's chart format, so old entries can turn out wrong and no one knows why yet.
- **Blood War:** devils and demons are known to the Federation, but the war between them is not. Glimpses of it can come gradually.
- **Outsiders:** beings outside the Federation don't know how ensouled ships work yet.

As episodes reveal facts, they move up this list.

## The ship: *Tranelis Selevarun*

- Ensouled vessel; formerly a male sea-elf bard. New to being a ship. He has a sense of humor. The rest of his personality is the writer's choice. Decide how long he lived before joining only if it's relevant to the story (a young soul and an old one would behave very differently). Speaks aloud. Doesn't need to sleep. Plays music, including *Orlalassar's Battle Theme* (all action) and *Oribella's Theme* (tragic undertone). Keeps his bawdy songs to the tavern, at the captain's request.
- What the crew call him (Ship, his name, initials, a nickname) is a storyline choice, not yet made. "Ship" in these files is only shorthand. The writer of Episode 1 picks, and the winning draft's choice becomes canon.
- At the destination, his portals open as a point of light, sometimes with musical accompaniment, that stretches into a line, then a rectangle, then a tall ornate golden door. The door is a bard's entrance, and he dresses it for the room. The gold and the opening sequence never change, but its ornament, scale and music change with whoever is watching and with his mood: flattering, teasing, solemn, or rough when he's in a hurry. He can also make it stealthy: small, dim, silent and unadorned, though still gold and still visible to anyone looking in the right place. He hates doing this, and he isn't very good at it yet. Otherwise, the door always shows something of his sea-elf past and usually carries some touch of his humor. The details are the writer's choice. On the ship's side, the portal is a plain opening.
- Runs most operations himself, so the bridge is usually empty except for the captain; full complement only for coordinated operations.
- Locations: bridge (outer ring of stations facing a central **scryer**), one room that serves as the captain's ready room, council chamber and briefing room, tavern, infirmary, druid grove, workshops, quarters, a shrine. Feel free to make up any other ship locations. Describe the ship's appearance only if it's relevant to the story.

## Arcs

Plots that run across several episodes. Each arc gets its own subsection with:

- **Premise**: the question or threat that drives it, in a sentence or two.

- **Where it's heading**: the planned turns and ending, if any. Mark anything a writer must not reveal yet.

An episode prompt names the arc (or arcs) the episode belongs to. Without one, the episode is a standalone.

## Images

Every episode comes with these images, in one consistent art style. Each must match what the story says about its subject. Where this file links to an image of a character, the ship, the door or the bridge, read its path against the base URL in the prompt, match that look and style, and give the image to your image tool as a reference if it accepts one. The door varies by design, so use its linked image only for the gold and the art style.

- **Cover**: a cover image for the episode, showing its title.
- **Every section**: one image for each section, scene or chapter of the story, shown at its start.
- **The dimension door**: the ship's golden door as it appears in this episode, at its destination, if he opens one.
- **Anything else**: any other moment you think an image would improve.
- **Whatever the prompt adds**: the prompt's own *Images* section lists more for this episode.

Generate the images yourself, named as listed in *4. Images* below. If you can't generate images, write the prompts anyway and mark them "not generated", so they can be made with another tool.

## Before you deliver

Check your work before you hand it over: reread the finished episode, end sections and images as a skeptical editor would, and fix what you find. Check:

- **Rules**: length (count the words; don't estimate), the prompt's required characters and scenes, terminology, and what people know (nothing listed as unknown is stated as settled without evidence).
- **Time and numbers**: redo the arithmetic. Stardates, elapsed days and nights, ages, distances and travel times must agree with each other and with the calendar.
- **Continuity**: who knows what and when, who is where, the order of events, and the same event told from two points of view.
- **Prose**: slips such as the wrong pronoun, a line a character refers back to but never said, or an abandoned sentence.
- **End sections**: they match the story, and everything you invented is listed under New canon.
- **Images**: every required image exists and matches the story.

## Delivery

Deliver everything as one downloadable tar file named `new.tar`, holding exactly this at its top level, with no enclosing folder:

- `new.md`: the episode and its end sections
- `new-images/`: the images
- `new.pdf`: a reading copy of the episode with the same text as `new.md`, opening with the cover, with section and scene images placed where they belong in the story, and with every other image either placed in the story or gathered at the end. It contains every image you generated and leaves out the end sections.

If you can't make the PDF, leave it out. If you can't make a tar file, put the episode and end sections in your reply and offer each image, and the PDF if you made one, as a separate download.

## After each episode

Every episode ends with these four sections, each under its own heading, after the story itself.

### 1. Metadata

A short table:

- Episode: the episode number this prompt asked for
- Model: the exact name and version of the model that wrote this. If you aren't sure, write "unknown" rather than guess.
- Effort: the reasoning or effort setting you ran at (for example low, medium, high, or max). If you can't tell, write "unknown".
- Title, and a one-sentence logline
- Stardate(s) covered
- Word count
- Planar system(s) and locations visited
- Point-of-view characters
- Themes
- Bible facts revealed: each fact from the bible that characters learned, and the evidence that showed it
- New canon introduced: every place, people, god, object, spell, or rule you invented, one line each, marked **storyline-only** or **world-level** (a world-level fact would belong in the bible)
- Open threads left for later episodes
- Self-check: what checking your work found and fixed, and any problem you knowingly left in

### 2. Character details

For every named character who appears, including any you invented, list what is true about them but **not shown in the story**: appearance details, D&D race (if appropriate), class and approximate level (if relevant; these notes are the only place a level appears), background, what they want, what they fear, secrets, and relationships to other characters. Mark anything that contradicts or extends this file.

### 3. Story beats by plot

Name each plot (A-plot, B-plot, and so on) with a one-line summary. Under each, list its beats in order. For each beat give: where it falls in the story (section or scene), the characters involved, and one or two sentences on what happens and what changes. End with a short note on how the plots intersect if they do.

### 4. Images

A table with one row per image:

- File: `cover.png`, `section-<NN>-<slug>.png` (numbered in story order), `door.png`, `ship.png`, `character-<name>.png` or `scene-<slug>.png`
- Subject: what it shows, and for a section or scene, where it falls in the story
- Status: generated or not generated
- Prompt: the full prompt, complete enough to reuse on its own in another image tool
