# Astral Trek Episode 1 prompt

Copy everything below the line into the AI.

---

You are writing **Episode 1 of Astral Trek**, an episodic fantasy series in the style of Star Trek, set in a multiverse called the Astral Garden that blends D&D 5e with Iain Banks' Culture. I designed the world. Your job is to write a story I'll enjoy reading.

## Read first

Read these files completely before you write anything. They are canon, and where this prompt is silent, they decide. (Inside the files, `bible.md` means the bible, `storylines.md` the storylines file, and `storylines/astral-trek/storyline.md` the Astral Trek storyline file.)

1. https://www.stdmn.com/astral-garden/bible.md: world rules (the astral sea, gods, souls, magic, the Federation, fiends).
2. https://www.stdmn.com/astral-garden/storylines.md: guidance for every story in the setting.
3. https://www.stdmn.com/astral-garden/storylines/astral-trek/storyline.md: format, ship, crew, and tone for Astral Trek.

## Characters I want

I want these two characters, as described in the storyline file:

- **Tranelis Selevarun** (Ship).
- **Kono**, the ship's faerie dragon mascot.

Make up everyone else: the captain, the rest of the crew, locals, villains, other ships. Give each new character a sound or smell when they first appear.

## Scenes I want

Include these scenes. Open the episode with the first one; place the second wherever it serves the story best.

1. **The captain's log.** The captain writes a log entry by hand in the logbook, signs it, closes the book, and stows it in a compartment in the captain's chair. Note that its twin logbook at Fleet headquarters has updated as well.
2. **Ship opens a portal.** Tranelis opens a portal (destination is your choice), the way he likes to make an entrance: a point of light that hisses, stretches into a line, then a rectangle, then a tall ornate golden door. Let the crew react to it.

Everything else (the planar system, the threat, the plot, the villain) is your choice. Surprise me.

## How to write it

- **Length:** up to an hour's read, at most 15,000 words. Aim for 10,000 to 12,000 words.
- **Structure:** your choice.
- **Point of view:** your choice.
- **Tone:** serious stakes, a warm crew, regular humor (Kono, Ship's showing off, the captain's knock-knock jokes). Not grimdark.
- **Theme:** reason in a world of magic. The climax is solved by someone understanding how a thing works, not only by hitting harder.
- **Craft:** every scene advances the story and is interesting on its own. Use sounds and smells. Short sentences for action, longer ones to slow down. Keep flashbacks and introspection brief.
- **Timeline:** year 1 of the Federation's calendar (stardates look like 1.693). The Federation is young, so ensouled ships are rare and new. Ships use drones; there are no avatars yet.
- **What the crew knows:** devils and demons are known, but the Blood War between them is not. The crew and Ship don't know the quiet circle exists (a reader may glimpse it in a cold open).
- **Terms:** say "whole soul", never "un-sundered". Shifting is an instant move between ships or planes; teleporting is an instant move within one plane or ship; portaling (a dimension door) opens a walk-through passage between ships or planes.
- **D&D:** characters' abilities should be recognizable as 5e classes and spells, but story comes first. Never weaken a scene to keep it rules-accurate.

## After the story

Once the story ends, add these three sections, each under its own heading.

### 1. Metadata

A short table:

- Model: the exact name and version of the model that wrote this. If you aren't sure, write "unknown" rather than guess.
- Effort: the reasoning or effort setting you ran at (for example low, medium, high, or max). If you can't tell, write "unknown".
- Title, and a one-sentence logline
- Stardate(s) covered
- Word count
- Planar system(s) and locations visited
- Point-of-view characters
- Themes
- New canon introduced: every place, people, god, object, spell, or rule you invented, one line each, marked **storyline-only** or **world-level** (a world-level fact would belong in the bible)
- Open threads left for later episodes

### 2. Character details

For every named character who appears, including any you invented, list what is true about them but **not shown in the story**: appearance details, D&D race, class and approximate level, background, what they want, what they fear, secrets, and relationships to other characters. Mark anything that contradicts or extends the storyline file.

### 3. Story beats by plot

Name each plot (A-plot, B-plot, and so on) with a one-line summary. Under each, list its beats in order. For each beat give: where it falls in the story (section or scene), the characters involved, and one or two sentences on what happens and what changes. End with a short note on how the plots intersect.
